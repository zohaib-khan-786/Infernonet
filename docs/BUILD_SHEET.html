B · Before first power

Do not fit these — your modules already have them

PartWhy it is unnecessaryHow to confirm
I²C pull-upsBME280, ADS1115, most PCF8574 and DS3231 breakouts already carry 4.7–10 kΩ on SDA/SCL. The BSS138 board carries its own for the LCD side.Power off. Measure SDA to 3V3. ~4.7–10 kΩ means they are fitted. Do not add a second set.

Do not economise on these three

  1. 1N4148 across the buzzer. A buzzer is an inductive load; switching it off spikes to tens of volts on a 5 V rail. That spike is created outside the buzzer, by your switching, so no module can contain it. Without it you get unexplainable sensor faults, brownouts and eventually a dead MOSFET.
  2. 1000 µF on the 5 V rail. Three loads share one USB feed: ESP8266 Wi-Fi spikes over 300 mA, the MQ-135 heater at ~150 mA, and the LCD plus buzzer. This belongs to the supply path, not to any module.
  3. The 100 nF decoupling. The disturbance comes from the inductance of the wire, so a cap soldered to the module is at the wrong end of it to respond.

Blocking items — resolve all four

  1. PCF8574 fitted. Without it the firmware reports Sensor Fault permanently and never produces a food verdict.
  2. DS3231 charging disabled. On the HW-084/ZS-042 boards, remove the resistor feeding the DS3231 VCH pin. Confirm with a meter that no current flows into a non-rechargeable CR2032.
  3. Level shifter orientation. LV → 3V3, HV → 5V, GND → common ground. LV/HV reversed destroys the translator.
  4. No 5 V on any GPIO. Trace it once by hand before applying power. The ESP8266 is 3.3 V only and there is no protection.

One clarification, because it trips up almost everyone

The I²C bus is not a component — it is the single shared SDA + SCL wire pair running from GPIO4/GPIO5. Five devices hang off it: BME280 (0x76), DS3231 (0x68), ADS1115 (0x48), the expander PCF8574 (0x20), and — through the level shifter — your LCD backpack (0x27). The RC522 is on a separate SPI bus, and the LEDs and buzzer are plain switch outputs, and only the buzzer has a transistor. The DHT11 is gone - humidity now comes off the BME280 on that same I2C bus, which frees GPIO16.
And note there are two PCF8574 chips, unrelated to each other: the one already soldered inside your LCD at 0x27, and the separate expander module you are buying at 0x20.

C · Build order — one stage at a time

Stage 1 · 3.3 V I²C bus

Wire SDA and SCL as a shared pair to BME280, DS3231, ADS1115, PCF8574. Every VCC to 3V3, every GND to common ground. Fit 100 nF at each module. Address straps: BME280 CSB→3V3 and SDO→GND (0x76) · ADS1115 ADDR→GND (0x48) · PCF8574 A0/A1/A2→GND (0x20).

Add 4.7 kΩ pull-ups SDA→3V3 and SCL→3V3 only if your modules carry none.

Stage 2 · (removed)

This stage used to wire the DHT11 to GPIO16. The DHT11 is removed - the BME280 measures humidity over the I2C bus you already built in stage 1, so nothing is wired here. GPIO16 (silkscreen D0) is spare and is the only non-boot-strapped pin the ESP8266 has to spare.

Reminder from stage 1: on NodeMCU, silkscreen D0 is GPIO16 and GPIO0 is D3. Different pins.

Stage 3 · RC522 on SPI

3.3V→3V3, GND→GND, SS/NSS→GPIO15, SCK→GPIO14, MOSI→GPIO13, MISO→GPIO12, RST→PCF8574 P5, IRQ unused.

Silkscreen D5=GPIO14, D6=GPIO12, D7=GPIO13, D8=GPIO15.

Stage 4 · Level shifter and LCD

LV→3V3, HV→5V, GND→common GND. LV1←GPIO4 (SDA) to HV1→LCD SDA. LV2←GPIO5 (SCL) to HV2→LCD SCL. LCD VCC→5V, GND→GND. LV3/LV4 unused.

Adjust the contrast pot only after power-on — the usual symptom is a solid block of blocks.

Stage 5 · MOSFET driver stage — build 4 identical

gate← PCF pin, source→GND, drain→load, 100 kΩ from gate to GND.

The gate pull-down is what keeps the buzzer silent during boot and if I²C hangs.

Stage 6 · Reed switch on P0

One terminal to P0, the other to GND, with 10 kΩ from P0 to 3V3. Closed must read LOW. If the switch is normally-closed, invert REED_CLOSED_LEVEL and re-test.

Stage 7 · MQ-135 into ADS1115 AIN0

MQ-135 VCC→5V with 100 nF across it, GND→GND, D0 unused. Then A0 → 4.7 kΩ → AIN0 node → 2.2 kΩ → GND. AIN1–AIN3 unused.

Measure A0 before fitting the divider. Expect 0.3–0.6 V on a 10 kΩ-load board, lower in a hot humid cabinet. Record the value and the board's load resistor below.

Acceptance criteria for "it works"

  1. No Sensor Fault in the reported status — the PCF8574 is answering at 0x20.
  2. Temperature and pressure both read live, not zero.
  3. Humidity reads live.
  4. MQ baseline captured, and the AIN0 value sits in the range recorded in stage 7.
  5. All three LEDs change with the status the device reports.
  6. Buzzer beeps on an alert, and stays silent when idle.
  7. LCD shows the status pages, with the contrast pot adjusted.
  8. RFID tag scan registers an inventory item and it survives a power cycle.
  9. Door open is detected and cleared correctly.
  10. [Backend] seq n accepted appears once the service URL and key are set.

F · Commissioning the backend link

  1. Start the service: cd backend && npm start. Confirm GET /healthz returns {"status":"ok"}.
  2. In the firmware set BACKEND_URL to the origin only — http://192.168.1.50:8080, no trailing slash and no /api suffix.
  3. Set BACKEND_INGEST_KEY to the plaintext whose SHA-256 is the service's FG_INGEST_KEY_SHA256. The service stores only the hash.
  4. For plain HTTP over the LAN, no TLS fingerprint is needed. If you use an https URL you must also set BACKEND_TLS_FINGERPRINT_ENABLED and supply a real fingerprint, or the firmware refuses to send.
  5. Open the dashboard at http://localhost:5173. The device appears within one interval (30 s).
